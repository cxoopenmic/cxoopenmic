The Open Mic format

Five minutes.
Something worth sharing.

A focused format keeps the attention on the person, the experience, and the takeaway. Speakers choose their own topics and perspectives.

01

Practitioners take the mic

Share a lesson from your work, a decision that changed your thinking, or a question you are still exploring.

02

Keep it focused

Five minutes. No decks. No sales pitches. Give the room enough context to understand the experience and something useful to consider.

03

Continue the conversation

Connect with peers around the ideas you heard. Compare experiences, ask questions, and find people facing similar challenges.

Open Mic Night / Practitioner talkIllustrative example

The pilot worked.
The handover taught us more.

Imagine a leader sharing what happened when an AI pilot became an everyday business service. The technology worked, but ownership became unclear.

The experience

The speaker describes the decision, the gap it exposed, and how the team responded.

The lesson

Agree who owns the service before the handover, including what happens when its use changes.

The question for the room

Where could responsibility become unclear in your own organisation?

An illustration of a talk, not a report of an actual event or a prescribed topic.

A simple way to shape your talk

  1. What happened?
  2. What did you learn?
  3. What can others take away?

Make it your own

Your experience is the starting point. A clear story and an honest lesson give the room something to work with.

Before the night

Bring your perspective.
Find your people.

Do I have to speak to attend?

You can take part by listening, asking questions, and connecting with peers. Check your community site for the details and participation options for each night.

How do I express interest in speaking?

Visit your community site for speaking opportunities, or contact our team with your city and the experience you would like to share.

Can I bring slides or a sales presentation?

The format is five minutes, no decks, and no sales pitches. Focus on your experience, the lesson, and what the room can take away.

How do Draft Nights and Encore fit in?
