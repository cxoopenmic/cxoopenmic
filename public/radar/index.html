The idea behind Radar

See the signal.
Understand the relevance.

Leadership attention is limited. Radar brings research, external developments, and permission-cleared community observations into a curated view of what deserves a closer look.

Use it to prepare for a decision, identify a question, or follow an emerging theme—whether or not you attend an Open Mic event.

01

Gather useful inputs

Bring together partner research, published developments, and questions from the CISO and CAIO communities. Keep the original source and date attached.

02

Assess the relevance

Explain who the signal affects, why it matters, and what the evidence supports. Distinguish a reported finding from an early observation or an open question.

03

Keep attention focused

Organize selected signals into leadership watchlists and emerging themes. Review, update, or retire items as the evidence changes.

a business service.

A proposed watchlist item on the transition from experimentation to ongoing use. This example demonstrates the format, not a verified finding.

What we’re looking at

How organizations assign ownership, oversight, and review responsibilities when an AI pilot moves into regular operation.

Why it matters

The transition connects security assurance with AI adoption and business accountability. It gives leaders a concrete point at which to examine their operating arrangements.

What to watch next

Published guidance, documented operating models, and permission-cleared observations that clarify how leaders handle this handover.

How is Radar different from Amplify?

Radar helps leaders identify what deserves attention. Amplify turns selected Radar insights into briefs and questions for executive conversations. Radar remains useful independently of an event.

How do research partners contribute?

Research and intelligence organizations contribute material to Radar within the agreed scope of each relationship. CxO retains editorial responsibility for selection and framing; sources retain their attribution.

Is every signal a confirmed trend?

No. A research finding, a single observation, and an emerging theme carry different evidence. Radar would identify those distinctions and show the limits of what can be concluded.
