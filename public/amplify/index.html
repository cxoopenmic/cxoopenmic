The pilot is ready to scale.
Who owns it next?

Building on the Radar signal, imagine an AI pilot is approved for everyday use. The room explores what needs to change before it becomes an ongoing business service.

The signal to investigate

Radar asks who owns the handover from pilot to service. This brief asks leaders to compare how they assign business ownership, security oversight, and responsibility for AI performance.

Why bring it to the room?

Leaders can compare how accountability works in practice: who makes the decision, who maintains visibility, and what happens when a use case changes.

AI governance Leadership & accountability

Demonstration copy, not a research finding. A published brief would identify the source organization, original publication, date, link, and any limitations or permissions.

Questions for the room

  1. Who owns an AI use case after the pilot ends?
  2. What must the business owner, CISO, and CAIO agree before the service goes live?
  3. What triggers a new review if the model, data, or purpose changes?

Does Amplify change the event format?

Amplify supports the existing five-minute, no-deck, no-sales-pitch format. Briefs offer context and optional prompts; they do not prescribe every talk or replace practitioner experience.

Who owns the research?

Sources keep their attribution. We would link to original work and use material within agreed permissions. Radar provides source selection and assessment; Amplify adds audience context and discussion framing.
