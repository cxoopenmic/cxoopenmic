The idea behind Resonance

Give the learning
a life beyond the room.

The most useful part of a conversation is often what it reveals: a shared challenge, a difference in experience, or a question nobody has resolved.

Resonance is CxO Open Mic’s original editorial synthesis of those observations, connected to the context that informed the discussion.

01

Listen for what matters

Gather observations that participants have agreed may be used. Preserve the context of the event and the limits of what was heard.

02

Connect without flattening

Identify recurring themes, points of disagreement, and questions worth pursuing. Separate observations from interpretation.

03

Publish a useful perspective

Share reviewed notes with clear scope, attribution, and open questions. Return permission-cleared observations and open questions to Radar, where they can inform future watchlists, conversations, and partner research.

who stays accountable?

Following the Amplify brief, this hypothetical note explores the distinction between approving a pilot and accepting responsibility for a live service. It does not report an actual event or participant statements.

A possible observation

A discussion might reveal that approving an AI pilot and assigning responsibility for its ongoing use are treated as separate decisions.

A tension to preserve

A CISO conversation might focus on control ownership; a CAIO conversation might focus on ownership of adoption and value. A useful note would examine these differences only where the conversations support them.

An editorial interpretation

A possible interpretation to test: launch approval and ongoing ownership need an explicit handover. If supported by the conversation, the note could examine a named owner, agreed review triggers, and responsibilities across business, security, and AI teams.

How does Resonance feed back into Radar?

Permission-cleared observations and unresolved questions can become inputs for Radar assessment. They retain their community context and evidence limits; an event observation does not automatically become a trend.

How is Resonance different from an event recap?

A recap describes the event. A Resonance Note develops a specific insight or tension, explains the evidence behind it, and identifies questions that remain. Photos and general event news can continue separately.

Will participants be quoted or named?

Only with appropriate permission and review. Anonymous synthesis also needs care: distinctive situations or details can identify a person or organization. Sensitive material should be left out unless cleared.

Does an observation become a trend?

Not automatically. Notes would distinguish a single experience, a recurring theme, and a wider interpretation. Claims about broader patterns need supporting evidence.
