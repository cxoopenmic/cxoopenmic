What happens at a Draft Night

Try it out.
Talk it through.

A useful idea gets clearer when you share it. Draft Nights bring practice and peer conversation together, helping you find the experience, question, or takeaway at the heart of your talk.

01

Bring something to explore

Start with an experience from your work, a lesson learned, or a question that other leaders might recognise.

02

Give it a first audience

Try out the idea or a draft talk. Hear what connects, what raises questions, and where a little more context would help.

03

Develop the next version

Take the conversation back into your thinking. Refine your message and build connections with people who bring different experiences.

Draft Nights / A talk in developmentIllustrative example

Our AI pilot worked.
The handover was harder.

Imagine a leader brings an early idea about moving an AI pilot into everyday use. There is a story to tell, but the key lesson is still buried in the details.

The first version

A broad account of the project: the technology, the launch, and the responsibilities that became unclear along the way.

What the conversation helps uncover

Peers ask about the moment ownership became unclear. That question gives the speaker a sharper focus: what the team should have agreed before the handover.

The next version

A focused story about one decision, what happened, and a question other leaders can take back to their teams.

An illustration of the experience, not an announced event or a report of an actual discussion.

Questions to develop the idea

  1. What happened that changed your thinking?
  2. Which detail would help another leader understand the decision?
  3. What should the audience remember or question afterwards?

Keep your own perspective

The story comes from your experience. Peer questions help you make it clearer and more useful to others.

Before you join

An idea is enough
to start a conversation.

Do I need a finished talk?

Draft Nights are for developing ideas and trying out talks. You can start with a practical lesson, a rough outline, or a question you want to explore.

How is this different from Open Mic Night?

Draft Nights focus on developing ideas, practice, and connections. Open Mic Night brings the community together around five-minute practitioner talks, with no decks and no sales pitches.

How does Encore fit in?

Encore continues selected conversations through focused dinners, roundtables, and summits. Together, Draft Nights, Open Mic Night, and Encore give ideas room to develop, reach the community, and go deeper.

How can I participate?

Contact the CxO Open Mic team with your city and the community or topic you are interested in. Ask about Draft Nights and how you can take part.
